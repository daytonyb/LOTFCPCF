First Impressions

Piggy's Behavior of Note:

In the beginning of the book, Piggy spots the conch and immediately recognizes its value. This displays the contrast between Piggy and the others because while Ralph sees it as a toy that makes a funny noise, Piggy sees it as a tool for organization on the island.


"We can use this to call the others. Have a meeting. They'll come when they hear us-" (Golding 16).

Psychological Interpretation of Piggy's Behavior:

I feel like this represents the anxiety that Piggy has about being on an island without an adults. When using the conch he insists on meetings and establishing order. Piggy uses this to try to "recreate" a more school-like environment, trying to rely on the strength of imaginary rules to protect him.


The psychological concept in use here is Social Anchoring. Piggy in this part of the book is scared of the unknown, and is using the conch to anchor him and his schoolmates to the "civilized" world.

My Prediction for how Piggy will act in the Future:

I think that Piggy will eventually become the "voice of reason" of the group, trying to brigde the gap between their island society and the civilized world that they left behind. I also feel like this level-headedness that Piggy will have will cause the more impulsive people it the group (Jack, the littluns) to find him threatening and annoying.
