Early Leadership Struggles

Piggy's Behavior of Note:

After Jack's hunters chase after a pig and let the signal fire go out, a ship passes by and misses the boys on the island. Piggy makes the unwise but courageous choice and ends up confronting Jack and asking him about his lack of responsibility.


"Jack smacked Piggy's head. Piggy's glasses flew off and tinkled on the rocks." (Golding 71).

Psychological Interpretation of Piggy's Behavior:

Piggy, while being technically right, is living under the false assumption that following the truth will save him. What Piggy failed to realize is that on their island civilization, "might makes right". The breaking of his glasses, while literally taking away some of his sight, also takes his "sight" away in a figurative way; Piggy's ability to see the world through a rational, logical, and scientific lens has been shattered.


This connects to the psychological idea of Scapegoating. Jack is unable to deal with the guilt and retribution that would await him for letting the fire go out, so he takes his anger out on the weakest person in the group: Piggy. Jack uses this to try to regain a feeling of power over the inhabitants of the island.

My Prediction for how Piggy will act in the Future:

Just like how Piggy's sight has diminished (both literally and figuratively), his influence over the inhabitants of the island will also diminish. As he realizes how powerless he really is on the island, he will also start to depend more on Ralph for physical protection.
