Fear and the Beast

Piggy's Behavior of Note:

During the late-night assembly, Piggy tries to argue against the "beastie" made up by the littluns by arguing that it doesn't make scientific sense. He also tries to convince them that the "beastie" isn't some monster in the woods, but is something imaginary coming out of the fear inside them all.


"I know there isn't no beast-not with claws and all that...but i know there isn't no fear either...unless we get frightened of people." (Golding 84).

Psychological Interpretation of Piggy's Behavior:

Piggy, being one of the smartest people on the island, realizes that the beast isn't an actual creature or "beastie" as the littluns call it, but instead is the violence within the boys themselves. By trying to prove that the "beastie" doesn't exist, he's using these scientific and logical arguments to protect himself from his own anxiety. He also uses this to try to preserve the overall sanity of the group.


Piggy uses the concept of Rationalization, a defense mechanism against irrational situations. When faced by the irrational situation of the littluns' fear of the "beastie", he rationalizes the situation by trying to use logical facts to explain it.

My Prediction for how Piggy will act in the Future:

As the group moves closer and closer to their "savage" instincts (or the Id), the logic behind Piggy's arguments (or the Super Ego) will get less and less effective. The others such as Jack and the littluns might start to make fun of him as they ignore the "facts" and give way to their fears of the "beastie".
