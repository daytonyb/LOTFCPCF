Moral Collapse

Piggy's Behavior of Note:

Jack's group raids Ralph's group, and steals Piggy's glasses so they can make a fire. Piggy, without his glasses, is left completely vulnerable. When faced with this challenge though, Piggy opts to become basically helpless, instead of using his usual logical and scientific approach to problems. Piggy starts to completely rely on Ralph to lead him.


"I thought they wanted the conch... They came for something else. Ralph-what am I going to do?" (Golding 168).

Psychological Interpretation of Piggy's Behavior:

Before the raid, Piggy was still thinking that the islanders would care about "rules". The raid allowed him to see that they care more about having "power". Piggy's mental health has also been declining throughout his time on the island, and losing his sight was the last straw. He no longer feels like he can control his life, and feels his influence over the island quickly dwindling.


Piggy displays the idea of learned helplessness. He's faced so many difficult situations and somehow came out of it, but this was too much for him to handle. With all the bullying Jack has done to him, Piggy now decides to give up on his logical approach and become completely dependent on others.

Prediction

At some point in the book, I think Piggy will try to confront Jack like he did when the fire went out. He will probably feel like it can't possibly get any worse than how it is now, leading him to make the bold but unwise decision to confront him.
