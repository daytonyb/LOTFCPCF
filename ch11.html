Final Psychological State

Piggy's Behavior of Note:

Piggy insists on going to Castle Rock and confronting Jack about his glasses. Piggy doesn't go to try to fight Jack, but goes back towards his logical approach towards problems, probably mixed with some hard emotions and hatred towards Jack, which helped lead him to make this impulsive decision. He goes and tries to tell Jack "what's right" to get his glasses back.


"I don't ask for my glasses back, not as a favor...but because what's right's right. Give me my glasses, I'm going to say-you got to!" (Golding 171).

Psychological Interpretation of Piggy's Behavior:

Piggy truly believes that "truth" and "justice" are universal laws. He thinks that just because giving his glasses back is the right thing to do, Jack will do it. It's still visible, even now at the end of the book, that he's still clinging to the notion that they are a part of the "civilized world", and refusing to admit that it could be any other way.


The psychological concept in use here is Moral Courage. Piggy probably realized that standing up to Jack would be dangerous for him. But he went and did it anyways. Piggy in this situation, clinging on to the old ways, chose to try to uphold the principles of right and wrong instead of keeping himself safe.

Final Thoughts:

Piggy died in the way that he lived his life on the island: clinging to the conch and the belief that humans are sensible. His death represents the loss of order and civilization on the island.
